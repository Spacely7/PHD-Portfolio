Flagship Research

A decision-support artefact for port activity monitoring, developed and evaluated through Design Science Research.

Built for an operational setting where large volumes of trade data rarely reach decision-makers as timely intelligence.

Problem

Data arrives faster than insight

Ports generate substantial operational and trade data, but converting it into timely, actionable intelligence remains difficult when analysis sits apart from the people making operational calls.

System

An integrated monitoring artefact

I developed a Power BI decision-support dashboard that consolidates analytical indicators, geographic patterns, commodity flows, and risk signals into one interactive environment.

Method

Predictive modelling on customs data

Ghana Customs Bills of Entry records — operational, financial, temporal, geographic, cargo, and risk attributes — were prepared, explored, and used to train a classification pipeline.

Validation

Compared across eight algorithms

Linear, tree, ensemble, kernel, and boosting methods were evaluated on accuracy, precision, recall, and F1, with explicit attention to what class imbalance does to each metric.

Key result

Prediction, explanation, decision

Feature-importance and SHAP analysis moved the work past ranking models by score. Knowing which attributes drive a flag is what makes a prediction reviewable by an officer rather than something to accept on trust.

Scope and limits

A proof of concept, stated as one

The artefact was constrained by the available dataset. Class imbalance, data availability, and the absence of live integration all remain open — and evaluating the system under realistic decision conditions is the necessary next step.

Explainable, Human-Centred Decision Support

An emerging doctoral direction that extends predictive analytics toward systems that reason alongside the people using them.

Emerging research direction

From prediction to reviewable decision support

My doctoral interest sits at the point where a model hands over to a person. That handover is where accuracy stops being the useful measure — what matters is whether the analyst can tell why the system produced this output, how confident it is, and when to override it.

How can intelligent decision-support systems integrate predictive analytics, explainable machine learning, and human judgement to support reliable decision-making in complex operational environments?
